Introduction

Global Burden of Lung Cancer

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Early detection is the single most impactful intervention for improving patient 5-year survival rates, yet the disease is frequently diagnosed at advanced, less treatable stages.

Limitations of Manual CT Interpretation

Chest CT scans are the gold-standard diagnostic modality. However, manual radiological interpretation is time-consuming, subjective, and prone to fatigue, creating severe diagnostic bottlenecks in high-volume screening programs.

Role of Deep Learning & Attention

Deep learning—specifically CNNs augmented with CBAM spatial/channel attention—automates lesion screening, eliminates subjective reading bias, and ensures consistent, scalable, and reproducible clinical decision support.

Project Scope

This project develops a clinically viable, end-to-end deep learning CT classification system distinguishing three diagnostic categories: normal parenchyma, benign nodules, and malignant carcinomas.

Methodological Focus

The core emphasis is on methodological rigor and clinical safety: strict patient-level grouped cross-validation, Class-Balanced Focal Loss, two-stage input screening, and quantitative Grad-CAM explainability.

⚑

The three-class distinction (normal / benign / malignant) provides actionable clinical triage and is significantly more challenging than binary tasks prevalent in prior literature.

Literature Review

PART I — 2025–2026 BENCHMARK STUDIES
Wang et al. (Clinics, 2026)
Meta-Review

Systematic review of 204 AI-based lung cancer studies. CNN was the most prevalent architecture (accuracy 68.4%–100%, AUC 0.61–1.00). Concluded that commercial deployment remains blocked by inconsistent study quality and lack of reporting standards.

Choudhury et al. (Proc. Comp. Sci., 2025)
98.7% Acc

Benchmarked CNN, VGG16, ResNet50, and InceptionV3 on IQ-OTH/NCCD (1,097 images). Reported 98.7% validation accuracy. Splitting methodology was completely omitted, leaving reproducibility and anatomical leakage unverified.

Ayesha et al. (JAIT, 2025)
99.5% Acc

Evaluated modified EfficientNet (B0–B7) with additional CNN attention layers on IQ-OTH, Chest-CT, and LIDC-IDRI. EfficientNetB1 achieved top performance. Images were shuffled and split at the slice level despite patient IDs being available.

PART II — METHODOLOGICAL CHALLENGES
Alpsalaz (GU J Sci, 2025)
98.0% Acc

Applied ResNet101, VGG19, and DenseNet121 on IQ-OTH/NCCD using SMOTE for class balancing. DenseNet121 showed significant confusion between benign and normal. Noted that SMOTE synthetic pixel artifacts degrade medical imaging realism.

Jian et al. (Scientific Reports, 2025)
99.77% Acc

Proposed a hybrid CNN-GRU combining spatial and sequential feature extraction on IQ-OTH. Achieved 99.77% on an 80:20 holdout. Authors explicitly note that cross-center generalizability and out-of-distribution robustness were untested.

Tan et al. (Arch. Comp. Meth. Eng., 2025)
PRISMA Survey

Surveyed 119 studies across 7 architecture families. Identified nodule-anatomy overlap (pleura, blood vessels) and lack of input validation as major unresolved clinical deployment hurdles.

Research Gap and Challenges

CRITICAL METHODOLOGICAL DEFICIT
Patient-Level Data Leakage

Most IQ-OTH/NCCD studies employ random slice-level train/test splitting. Because the dataset contains only 110 unique patients across ~1,097 slices, multiple consecutive slices from the same patient appear in both training and test sets.

The model simply memorizes individual patient anatomy (rib curvature, bone density) rather than learning true pathological cancer biomarkers, producing artificially inflated accuracies.

Confirmed Leakage in Prior Works
  • • Ayesha et al.: 80:20 slice-level split with shared patients
  • • Alpsalaz: 75:25 image-level split without patient grouping
  • • Jian et al.: 80:20 holdout with slice shuffling
1. Absence of Input Modality Validation

Existing pipelines process any uploaded file blindly. Clinical safety requires verifying that the input is a genuine medical CT and contains thoracic lung anatomy before inference.

2. Synthetic Pixel Artifacts (SMOTE Issues)

Prior works use SMOTE interpolation, generating synthetic pixel noise that disrupts delicate radiomic CT textures. Mathematical loss reweighting (Focal Loss) is clinically superior.

3. No Multi-Center Cross-Dataset Testing

Models trained and tested on a single hospital's scans collapse on external data due to scanner resolution and field-of-view discrepancies (full chest vs. nodule crops).

4. Lack of Quantitative Explainability

Visual heatmaps are rarely validated mathematically. Proving clinical trust requires testing whether attention peaks land inside radiologist-annotated tumor contours.

Methodological Innovations Across Each Stage:
  • Two-Stage Validation: Rejects non-CT images and non-thoracic organs (brain, abdomen) before feeding model.
  • Letterbox Padding: Preserves natural circular nodule geometry without aspect-ratio distortion.
  • Linear Histogram Matching: Standardizes external scanner intensities (μ=101.5, σ=75.6) to eliminate manufacturer bias.
  • Class-Balanced Focal Loss: Focuses backpropagation on hard tumor margins without generating fake SMOTE noise.
  • Explainable Verification: Quantitative Pointing Game verifies AI focus against board-certified radiologist masks (93.8% hit rate).

Collected from Iraq-Oncology Teaching Hospital & National Center for Cancer Diseases. Publicly hosted on Mendeley Data.

LIDC-IDRI — External Test Dataset
Multi-Center Validation
📁 750 Clean Slices ⚕ 4-Radiologist Consensus

International reference cohort from 7 academic centers (The Cancer Imaging Archive, TCIA).

Clean Consensus Protocol: 250 confirmed Normal, 250 Benign, and 250 Malignant solid tumor slices (≥3 radiologist agreement) with verified contour masks.

Class Imbalance Resolution: Why Focal Loss Replaced SMOTE
1. The Flaw of SMOTE in Medical CT

SMOTE synthesizes minority samples by interpolating between feature points. In CT scans, this generates blurred, unphysical pixel noise that violates anatomical Hounsfield Unit densities and corrupts radiomic textures.

2. Class-Balanced Focal Loss (γ=2.0)

Operates purely in loss computation: down-weights easy, well-classified normal slices and scales up the gradient contribution of hard benign/malignant lesion boundaries without modifying pixel integrity.

Research Methodology — Preprocessing & Augmentation

CLINICAL PREPROCESSING PIPELINE
➜ Two-Stage Automated Validation

Stage 1 verifies medical grayscale modality (rejects color photos). Stage 2 segments lung cavities and spine (rejects brain, abdomen, and non-thoracic scans).

➜ Letterbox Padding & Resizing (240×240)

Preserves square aspect ratio with uniform borders, preventing round nodules from stretching into ovals. Resized smoothly to EfficientNet-B1's native resolution.

➜ Linear Histogram Matching

Standardizes pixel intensity distributions (μ=101.5, σ=75.6) across external hospital scanners to remove machine manufacturer contrast bias.

➜ Tensor Normalization

Scaled to [0, 1] and normalized with ImageNet mean/std for stable gradient backpropagation and pre-trained transfer learning compatibility.

AUGMENTATION & INFERENCE PIPELINE
Physics-Informed Training Augmentation

Applies horizontal flips (leveraging bilateral lung symmetry), random affine rotations (±15° for patient couch tilt), and contrast jitter (±15%) to prevent spatial memorization.

Physical CT Film Sheet Processor

Custom OpenCV module detects film sheet borders, removes background light-box glare, rectifies perspective skew, and automatically crops out individual CT tiles for batch inference.

Test-Time Augmentation (TTA Ensembling)

Inference passes each scan through 5 augmented views (identity, flip, ±5° rotation, contrast shift) and averages softmax predictions, boosting external stability by 2%–5%.
